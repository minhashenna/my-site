# Shreya Iyer — Brand Strategist
## Independent Review

---

### Verdict: Promising bones. But it sounds like it was written *about* Henna, not *by* her.

---

### What's Working

The hero headline — *"A thought is running you right now"* — is the strongest line on the page. It's specific, present-tense, and it doesn't flinch. The before/after table is also genuinely good brand work: concrete, honest, no wellness fluff. The design is clean and the palette is distinctive without being precious.

The Byron Katie quotes are well-placed. They lend authority without being borrowed crutches — they serve the copy rather than replacing it.

---

### What's Not Working

**1. The About section loses the voice it earned.**

The hero has personality. Then we hit the About section and it suddenly sounds like a LinkedIn summary someone edited for warmth. *"I'm a former corporate professional — fifteen years across accounting, strategy, and marketing."* That's a resume line. Henna's story is more interesting than her titles. What changed? What cracked open? What did the 13 years of raising kids *do* to her understanding of the mind? The site tells me *what* she studied. It doesn't make me feel why any of it matters.

**2. The credential tags are a red flag.**

"Enneagram. Advaita Vedanta. NVC. Guru Granth Sahib." These are beautiful traditions — but displayed as green pills in a row, they read like a spiritual resume. Anyone who doesn't know these traditions won't be impressed. Anyone who *does* know them will wonder if she's a tourist or a practitioner. Neither reaction is what you want. These need to be *earned* in the copy, not listed.

**3. "Do the Work with Henna" lacks a human pulse.**

The paid service card is the darkest, most prominent card on the page — but it's also the most generic. *"I know where the mind deflects — and when to wait."* That line is close to something real. But every other line could describe any coach. What's *Henna's* specific way of facilitating? What does a session with her feel like that a session with someone else doesn't?

**4. No proof of Henna as a *person* anywhere on the page.**

No photo. No first-person story that breaks through. No specific moment — not even one — from her own life that shows this work actually touched her. The testimonials are placeholders. So the entire About section is asking visitors to trust a shape without a face.

**5. The section headers are too clever.**
"Two ways in." "Five steps. One sitting." "The work, in their words." These are witty — and uniformly so. The cleverness starts to feel formulaic after the second or third one. Real voice is uneven. It has a moment of precision and then a moment of plainness. Everything here is polished to the same sheen.

---

### Would I Remember This Site?

The *hero* and the *table* — yes. The About section — no. That's a problem, because the About section is where the brand actually lives.

---

### One Specific Fix

The About section needs one raw, specific story. Not a paragraph about what she studied. One moment: *I was standing in my kitchen at 8pm, furious at my kid for the same thing I'd been furious about for three years — and I finally asked: is this true? And the whole thing fell apart.* That's the brand. That's what makes someone book a session.

---

**Overall: 6.5/10.** The concept is sharp. The voice needs to arrive.
