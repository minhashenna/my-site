# Ankit Verma — Growth Marketer
## Independent Review

---

### Verdict: The top of funnel is strong. The conversion path is broken.

---

### 5-Second Test

**What is this?** — Clear within 2 seconds. "A thought is running you right now" + "self-inquiry process" + Byron Katie. I get it.

**Who is it for?** — Implied, not stated. The visitor has to recognize herself in the hero copy. Works for warm traffic (people who already know Byron Katie or have heard of Henna). Riskier for cold traffic.

**What do I do next?** — Two buttons in the hero: "Get a Reality Check" and "Do the Work with Henna." Clear enough. But clicking "Get a Reality Check" takes you to *a description of the process* — not the process itself. That's a broken promise and it's the single biggest conversion leak on the page.

---

### Funnel Analysis

**Awareness → Interest: Strong (8/10)**

The hero does its job. The before/after table is a high-converting format for this type of offer — specific, relatable, outcome-focused. The Byron Katie association adds instant credibility for the target audience. This section would hold attention.

**Interest → Consideration: Weak (4/10)**

After the table, the page goes: About → Services → How It Works → Testimonials → CTA → Contact. That's a long journey before asking for anything. The About section (which should build trust and deepen consideration) is currently undermined by the photo placeholder and the LinkedIn-style opening. A visitor who doesn't already trust Henna has nothing concrete to hold onto here.

**Consideration → Intent: Broken (2/10)**

The "Get a Reality Check" CTA — repeated three times across the page — links to an anchor section with a five-step static list. There is no actual tool. No form. No worksheet. No interactive process. The site is selling a free self-guided experience that doesn't exist yet. That's the single biggest conversion killer. A visitor who clicks expecting to *do* something and finds a read-only list will leave.

**Intent → Action: Unreachable (1/10)**

The contact form doesn't submit anywhere. There's no email address. There's no booking link for the paid session. No pricing. The visitor who is ready to pay has no clear path to do so.

---

### Specific Drop-off Points

1. **Hero CTA → "Get a Reality Check":** Clicks through to static content. Immediate bounce risk for anyone expecting the tool.

2. **Services section — paid card:** No price. No session length. No "what happens after I click." "Book a Session" goes to the contact form, which doesn't work. The entire paid offering has no conversion path.

3. **Testimonials:** All three are `[Name], [City]` placeholders. Social proof that reads as fake is worse than no social proof. This actively damages credibility at the exact moment a visitor is considering whether to trust the site.

4. **CTA banner:** Good copy. But "Get a Reality Check — free →" still links to the static How It Works section. Repetition of the broken promise.

---

### What's Missing for Conversion

| Missing element | Impact |
|---|---|
| The actual Reality Check tool (or waitlist) | Critical — the entire free offer is promised but absent |
| Pricing for paid session | High — creates ambiguity that kills intent |
| Real testimonials | High — placeholders signal an unproven product |
| Working contact/booking path | Critical — no revenue path exists |
| Email capture | Medium — no way to follow up with interested visitors |
| Who specifically this is for | Medium — cold traffic won't self-select |

---

### What to Do Before Launch

**Non-negotiable:**
1. Either build the tool or replace the CTA with "Join the waitlist" / "Get notified when it launches"
2. Connect the contact form to a real email (Formspree is free and takes 10 minutes)
3. Add pricing to the paid session card — even "Starting at $X" reduces friction
4. Remove or replace the placeholder testimonials

**Would significantly lift conversion:**
5. Add a one-line "This is for you if…" statement near the hero
6. An email capture (even just "Get the process as a PDF") builds a list from day one

---

### Would I Convert?

If I were the target customer and found this organically: **probably not today.** The interest is there. The path to action isn't. I'd close the tab meaning to come back, and I wouldn't.

If Henna sent me this link personally: **yes, I'd fill out the form** — but only because the personal context fills in the gaps the site doesn't.

---

**Overall: 5/10.** Strong top of funnel. Conversion infrastructure doesn't exist yet.
