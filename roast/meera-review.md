# Meera Nambiar — Target Customer
## Independent Review

*I'm 41. I work in HR at a tech company. I've been in therapy on and off for years. I journal. I do yoga. I've heard of Byron Katie but never actually sat down and done The Work. I'm smart, I overthink, and I'm currently in the middle of a low-grade resentment toward my mother-in-law that I can't seem to shake. I found this site through a friend's Instagram story.*

---

### My Honest Experience Reading This Site

---

**The headline stopped me.**

*"A thought is running you right now."* 

Yes. That's exactly what's happening. I actually looked up from my phone for a second. That line worked on me.

**Then I kept reading and I slowed down.**

The paragraph below the headline is good — the argument, the resentment, the replay. But then it shifts into explaining what the thing *is* in kind of flat, product-description language: *"a structured self-inquiry process that takes you from triggered to clear."* The word "structured" made me think of a worksheet. Which — I'll be honest — made me feel a little tired before I even started.

**The before/after table is the best thing on the page.**

*"Certainty that they're wrong → Curiosity about what's true."* That one hit me. I'd share that table. I paused there and read all five rows. If I were going to tell a friend about this site, that table is what I'd screenshot.

**Then I hit the About section and I wanted to know: who is Henna?**

The grey box where her photo should be is the first moment I felt something drop. I needed a face. I needed to know if this is someone I'd feel comfortable crying in front of, basically. "Fifteen years in corporate" isn't what I came here for. I wanted to know: what was the moment she stopped being able to manage her own mind with effort and strategy? What cracked? That's the story I needed.

I also didn't know what to do with "Advaita Vedanta" and "Guru Granth Sahib." I Googled one of them. I'm sure they're meaningful. But in that moment they made me feel like I might be missing some background to understand what I'm getting into.

**The services section answered some questions — but raised one big one.**

The free tool — fine, I'd try it. But the "Do the Work with Henna" card told me nothing about what a session actually looks like. How long is it? Is it on Zoom? Do I need to prepare anything? What does Henna *do* in the session — does she ask me questions, or do I just talk? The "Book a Session" button goes to a contact form, which feels like a lot of friction for someone who just wants to know: *what am I actually signing up for?*

And there's no price. Which made me feel slightly wary, the same way I feel when a menu says "market price." It doesn't feel exclusive — it just feels like I'm about to be surprised.

**The testimonials made me uneasy.**

[Name], [City]. Three of them. I understand these are placeholders but this is the moment where I most need to hear from someone like me. *"I'd just gotten used to it. The turnarounds broke something open."* That one is close — but without a name and a real person behind it, it feels like the site is describing itself. Which is what every site does.

**My unanswered questions after reading the whole page:**

1. Is this for people who already know Byron Katie, or can I come with zero background?
2. What's the difference between doing the free tool and doing a session with Henna — functionally, not just emotionally?
3. How long does a session take?
4. What does it cost?
5. What if I start the free process and get stuck?
6. Is Henna certified in anything? Has she worked with a teacher?

**Would I book?**

Not today — because the path isn't clear enough and there's no price. But I'd save the link. I'd probably share the before/after table. And if a friend told me they'd done a session with Henna and it helped, I'd come straight back.

The site has the right feeling. It just leaves me with too many open practical questions to say yes right now.

---

**What would make me book immediately:** A real photo of Henna. One real testimonial with a name. A price (even approximate). And a sentence that tells me explicitly — "you don't need any background in The Work to start."

---

**Overall experience: Interested but not ready.** Needed 20% more human and 20% more practical.
