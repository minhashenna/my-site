# Rahul Khanna — Angel Investor
## Independent Review

---

### Verdict: The positioning is defensible. The execution doesn't yet prove it.

---

### What I'm Evaluating

I'm not evaluating this as a venture-scale startup. I'm evaluating this as a founder's first public-facing asset — and asking: does this person know their market, have a point of view, and have the credibility to execute? Those three questions are what a first check comes down to.

---

### Market Clarity: Good

The self-guided mental wellness market is crowded — Headspace, Calm, BetterHelp, a thousand coaches. But this site does something none of those do: it takes a well-established, proven methodology (The Work of Byron Katie, used globally, with a decades-long track record) and makes it immediately accessible in a structured digital format. That's a real wedge. The decision to position *against* positive thinking and reframing ("this isn't reframing") is smart — it differentiates from the wellness mainstream and speaks directly to a more sophisticated buyer.

The two-tier model (free tool + paid sessions) is a standard but solid SaaS-influenced structure for a services business. Free tool builds trust and audience; paid sessions monetize the converted segment. Good instinct.

---

### Point of View: Present, not yet sharp enough

The copy has a perspective: inquiry over insight, reality over story, the triggered moment as the right moment. These are clear and consistent throughout the page. That's better than most coach websites, which have no perspective at all.

What's missing is the *contrarian claim* — the one thing Henna believes that most people in this space would push back on. The Work has been around since the 1980s. Henna is offering a digital-first, self-serve version of it. What does she believe about *why* that works better than a retreat, a book, or a certified facilitator? That argument would sharpen the positioning considerably.

---

### Credibility: Thin

This is the biggest issue from a bet-on-the-founder perspective.

**What's present:**
- 15-year corporate background (relevant: she understands structure, she has life experience)
- 13 years parenting (relevant: she has skin in the game with real relationships)
- A list of traditions studied

**What's absent:**
- No evidence she has *facilitated* anyone else through this process
- No photo (I can't see who I'm evaluating)
- No testimonials (three prominent placeholders damage credibility — they signal an unproven product)
- No mention of any training, certification, or supervised practice in The Work specifically
- No mention of how long she's been doing this work personally or professionally
- No origin story that shows *when* she committed to this — not just studied it

I'd want to know: has she facilitated 10 people through The Work? 100? Has she been trained by Katie's School for The Work? Does she have a supervisor? These aren't bureaucratic credentials — they're proof of commitment and proof of capability. Right now, the site asks me to trust her based on the depth of her *reading list*, not the depth of her *practice*.

---

### Business Viability: Questions remain

**No pricing.** I don't know if this is a $75 session or a $500 one. Pricing communicates positioning. Its absence signals either ambivalence or unreadiness.

**No funnel.** The free tool (the core product!) doesn't exist yet. The paid session has no booking path. The form doesn't submit. There's no email capture. This is a beautiful landing page for a business that has not yet been built.

**No sense of scale or ambition.** Is this a lifestyle practice — Henna seeing 10 clients a month? Or is there a larger vision (the AI version of Byron Katie she mentions in her background)? I don't need this to be a unicorn. But I'd want to know if the founder has a 3-year picture in their head.

---

### What Would Make Me Take a Meeting

1. **Two or three real clients who've done sessions with her** — even early, even free — whose names she can share
2. **A working version of the free tool**, even a PDF or a simple form-based version
3. **A clear answer to: "Why you, specifically, and not the 200 other certified Byron Katie facilitators?"**
4. **A photo and a voice** — I need to see and hear the founder before I have a view on whether she can build an audience

---

### Would I bet on this founder?

Based on the site alone: not yet, but I'd take the call. The concept is real. The market is real. The positioning instinct is right. What I need to see is that she's already doing the thing she says she wants to build — not preparing to do it.

*"She's still at the starting line"* is what the site communicates right now. What I want to see is: *"she's already running."*

---

**Overall: Intriguing concept, underbuilt proof. Would revisit after she has 10 paying clients.**
